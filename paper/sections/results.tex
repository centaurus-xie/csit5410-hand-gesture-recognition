\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}

The expanded experiment starts with 2,080 original photographs and 180
augmented derivatives. All variants of an original photograph belong to a
single group. After landmark extraction, we assign original groups within
each class to training, validation, and test partitions (approximately
70/15/15, seed 42). We include derivatives only when their original is
assigned to training. The resulting split contains 1,268 training images
(1,166 originals and 102 derivatives), 249 validation originals, and 249 test
originals. The other 59 usable derivatives are excluded because their
originals are held out. Each classifier uses the same split. Signer identifiers are
unavailable for the inherited data, so the partition controls for the
original photo and may include the same signer on both sides.

We report landmark yield over all inputs, classification accuracy over
the retained test images, and macro-$F_1$ over the 11 classes. Macro-$F_1$
gives each class equal weight. These metrics use the model's highest-scoring
class before confidence filtering or temporal confirmation.

\subsection{Landmark yield}

The detector retained 1,825 of 2,260 images (80.8\%). It detected zero hands
in 430 images and two hands in five. Restricting the denominator to original
photographs gives 1,664 of 2,080 (80.0\%). Yield varies by class
(Fig.~\ref{fig:yield}). The detector retained 120 of 206 original photographs
for digit 2 (58.3\%), compared with 207 of 207 for digit 5. Digit 9 has only
five usable originals. These differences affect the signal available to the
classifier and show why the classifier score alone cannot characterize the
full recognition system.

Table~\ref{tab:models} shows that the effect of explicit geometry depends on
the classifier and metric. Geometry alone improves test macro-$F_1$ over
coordinates alone for both models in this split. Concatenating coordinates
and geometry gives the MLP the highest test accuracy and macro-$F_1$ among
the six configurations. For the SVM, concatenation improves accuracy relative
to geometry alone while lowering macro-$F_1$. The coordinate-only MLP has a
higher validation macro-$F_1$ than the combined MLP. Given the small support for
some test classes and the different ordering across splits, the measurements
support reporting the comparison for this dataset, while the relative merit
of the inputs needs confirmation with additional signers.

The browser uses the combined MLP. It correctly classifies 246 of the
249 retained test photographs. Its three errors are $0\to9$, $1\to5$ and
$3\to4$. All 47 test examples from the two non-target pose families are
assigned to \texttt{unknown}. The test has one photograph for each of digits
6, 7 and 9. Each is correct on the test split, and the sole validation
examples for 7 and 9 are misclassified. Classes with one test example can
therefore have a large effect on macro-$F_1$.

As a deployment diagnostic, we apply the browser's per-frame rule to the two
held-out image splits. The rule emits a digit when the maximum probability is
at least 0.8 and its label is a digit. On the test split it returns 201
digit outputs, of which 198 are correct (98.5\% of emitted digits). One
digit photo is rejected, and all 47 non-target images abstain
(Table~\ref{tab:reject}). This check evaluates still images. A measurement
of live confirmation and false audio triggers requires sequences from new
users. The browser and Python implementations agree within $2.22\times10^{-16}$
in maximum absolute probability error on ten exported validation vectors.

\subsection{Extension to isolated video signs}

The separate video path reads a manually delimited clip and classifies an
isolated sign from hand landmarks alone. The prototype vocabulary uses
filming references from HKU-HKSLLEX~\cite{hku_hksllex} for THANK-YOU,
NEXT-TIME and GOODBYE. We resample 20 geometric features and two camera-plane
wrist displacements at 16 time points. Wrist displacements are relative to
the first valid frame and scaled by median palm size. The resulting
352-element vector is the input to a small MLP. The current verification uses
synthetic landmark sequences for feature and prediction parity. The
comparison to a single-frame baseline on recorded, signer-disjoint video
clips remains an experimental objective.
